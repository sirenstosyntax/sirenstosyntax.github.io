Your people are already using AI.

Chances are someone in your department has used a chatbot to draft a report, a policy, or a public statement. Most of the time nobody asked first, because there was no rule to ask about. That leaves a chief with questions that are hard to answer.

  • What incident, patient, or personnel information has gone into a public AI tool?
  • Who checks AI-written work before it goes into a record or out to the public?
  • What do you tell the council, the union, or a reporter who asks how your department uses AI?

A ban doesn't settle it. People keep using the tools on their own phones, and you lose the ability to see it. A clear policy and a short list of approved uses bring it into the open, where you can manage it.

How I can help.

Not sure which fits? Start with a conversation and we'll sort it out together.

  • AI readiness review

    I find out where AI is already in use across your department, what information is going into it, and where the real exposure is. You get a short written summary with the first things to fix.

  • Policy and governance

    I help you build an AI policy that fits your department instead of a template pulled from the internet. It covers which tools are approved, what information stays out of them, who reviews AI-assisted work, and how the policy gets updated as the tools change.

  • Practical adoption

    I help you pick the administrative work where AI saves the most time, such as reports, accreditation documentation, training material, and grant writing, and put it to use with guardrails your staff can follow.

  • Briefings and training

    I brief command staff and train company officers and administrative staff on what AI does well, where it fails, and what your policy expects of them. I also speak at conferences and chiefs' meetings.

  • Vendor questions

    When a vendor pitches an AI feature in software you already buy, I help you ask the questions that show what it does with your data and how its output gets checked.
